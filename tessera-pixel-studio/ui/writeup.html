Tessera: painting pixel art with a diffusion language model

Tessera treats a 16×16 sprite as a 256-word sentence. Every pixel is a token from a 512-color vocabulary. A bidirectional transformer starts from a canvas where every pixel is [MASK] and fills in the whole picture in parallel over about 16 steps, conditioned on your prompt. It has no VAE, no latent space and no autoregression: the model writes palette tokens directly.

This page explains every piece: how pixels become tokens, how the masked-diffusion objective works, what the network looks like, how it was trained, and how the sampler turns a blank canvas into a sprite. The figures use the real training logs of the model running in this Space.

1. Pixels as tokens

A diffusion language model needs a vocabulary. Pixel art is unusually well suited to this: sprites use a small set of flat colors, hard edges and no anti-aliasing, so a discrete palette loses almost nothing. The training set, Pixel Art Atlas 1.4M, contains 4,096 distinct colors and binary alpha. About 40% of all pixels are transparent.

Building a perceptual palette

  • Count every color across the whole dataset (a chunked GPU histogram over 24-bit RGB).
  • Weighted k-means in OKLab, a perceptually uniform color space, with weights count0.5. Square-rooting the counts keeps rare-but-important hues (a gem highlight, a flame tip) from being swallowed by dominant ones.
  • Snap every centroid to the nearest real dataset color, so exact pure colors (black outlines, white glints) survive untouched.
  • Token 0 is transparent, leaving 511 opaque colors. Every pixel in the dataset is then mapped to its nearest palette entry through a precomputed 16.7M-entry lookup table.

The mean quantization error is ΔEOK = 0.010, below the roughly 0.02 just-noticeable difference and about 4× better than a uniform 8×8×8 RGB cube (0.039).

2. Masked (absorbing-state) discrete diffusion

Instead of adding Gaussian noise to continuous values, a masked diffusion model corrupts a sequence by replacing tokens with a special [MASK] token. The model learns to predict the original token at every masked position, all at once. This is the family behind MaskGIT, MDLM and LLaDA, and the “diffusion LLMs” such as Mercury and DiffusionGemma.

xt = mask(x0, r)    r = cos(π/2 · u),   u ~ U(0,1)   ⇒   L = E[ meani ∈ masked −log pθ(x0,i | xt, prompt) ]

The mask ratio follows a cosine schedule. It is the same schedule the sampler uses to decide how many pixels to reveal at each step, so training and inference see the same distribution of partially painted canvases. Several refinements make the objective work better for sprites:

  • Stratified mask ratios across the batch (low-discrepancy sampling) and exact-count masks (exactly ⌈r·256⌉ pixels) reduce gradient variance.
  • Blob masks: 25% of masks are spatially correlated (low-frequency noise ranking), so the model also learns to fill the contiguous holes that confidence-ordered decoding leaves behind.
  • Perceptual label smoothing: 10% of the target mass is spread over perceptually close colors, weighted by softmax(−ΔE²/τ²) with τ = 0.05. A slightly-off shade is penalized less than a wrong hue.
  • Self-conditioning with look-back (50% of steps): a first pass predicts a draft distribution from a noisier canvas (30% extra masking), and its 512-way probabilities are fed back into the second pass, mimicking the sampler, which feeds the previous step’s predictions forward.
  • Classifier-free guidance training: the prompt is replaced by a learned null embedding 10% of the time. 5% of the time the short subject label is used instead of the full caption.
  • Caption-aware flips: horizontal flips (p = 0.5) are skipped when a caption mentions “left” or “right”.

3. Architecture

The denoiser is a small diffusion transformer with full 2D awareness. Text and pixels live in one joint, bidirectional sequence, as in a diffusion LLM: [4 registers | 24 text tokens | 256 pixel tokens]. The text tokens are updated by every block alongside the pixels.

The pieces, and why each is there

ComponentWhat it does
Color-grounded embeddingsemb(c) = learned(c) + MLP(Fourier(OKLab(c))). All 512 color tokens share statistical strength through their color coordinates, so a rare shade starts out close to similar common ones.
Dual-resolution hourglassPixel-level blocks handle outlines and dithering. A 2×2-merged core handles global shape and meaning at about a third of the cost. A zero-initialised skip path carries fine detail across the core.
2D axial RoPERotary position encoding on the (row, column) axes with θ = 100, suited to a 16-pixel world. Patch tokens use the sub-pixel coordinates of their 2×2 centre, so both levels share one geometric frame.
QK-RMSNormNormalising queries and keys keeps attention logits bounded, which allows the high Muon learning rate without loss spikes.
Conv-SwiGLUA zero-initialised depthwise 3×3 convolution on the gate branch of the MLP gives a strong local, translation-equivariant prior for very few parameters.
adaLN-singleOne conditioning MLP (pooled text + Fourier features of the mask ratio) produces shift, scale and gate for every block, plus a small learned per-block bias, as in PixArt-α. Zero-initialised gates make each block start as the identity.
RegistersFour learned tokens that absorb attention sinks, so pixel tokens do not have to.
Self-conditioning projectionA zero-initialised linear map from the previous step’s 512-way color distribution into the masked positions’ embeddings.
Why a joint sequence instead of cross-attention? In a diffusion LLM the prompt is just the start of the sequence. Letting text tokens attend to the evolving canvas, and be updated by it, gives the model a cheap way to re-read the prompt in light of what has already been painted, with no extra cross-attention layers.

4. Training recipe

The pipeline was built to keep one GPU busy. The entire tokenized dataset (about 1.2 GB of integer tensors) lives on the GPU, and batches are random gathers: there is no DataLoader and no CPU bottleneck. Everything runs in bf16 autocast under torch.compile with static shapes. The loss covers all positions with a 0/1 weight, so the compiled graph has no data-dependent shapes.

5. Parallel decoding: from blank canvas to sprite

Generation starts with all 256 pixels masked. Each step runs one batched forward pass and then commits a growing number of pixels:

  1. Predict a 512-way color distribution for every pixel, conditional and unconditional in one batch.
  2. Guide: logits = luncond + w · (lcond − luncond), with w = 3 by default (constant, or ramped).
  3. Sample a color for every masked pixel (temperature and top-p are optional).
  4. Choose which pixels to keep: rank them by log-confidence plus annealed Gumbel noise, so the order is mostly confident-first but not greedy. A cosine schedule fixes how many stay masked after each step.
  5. Spatial NMS: an uncertain pixel may only be committed if it is the most confident masked pixel in its 3×3 neighbourhood. Committing two adjacent pixels from independent marginals is the main failure mode of parallel decoding: they can disagree and leave speckles. Confident pixels (p ≥ 0.9, mostly background) are committed freely.
  6. Self-condition: the full predicted distribution is fed into the next step.

Inpainting comes free: pass a canvas plus a keep-mask, and kept pixels are never changed. The Pixel Editor in this studio uses it for the Magic brush, for “Remix”, and for completing a hand-drawn sketch. Optional polish passes re-mask the least confident 8% of pixels after decoding and re-predict them with full context.

The CLIP score (ViT-B/32 cosine × 100 between a sprite and its caption, on 512 validation prompts) of generated sprites matches that of the real dataset sprites for the same captions. CLIP is a coarse judge of 16×16 images, so this mainly shows that prompt adherence is at the level of the training data. It does not show the model beats it.

7. Limitations and honest notes

  • 16×16 only. The canvas size is fixed by the training data.
  • Captions are machine-written. The dataset card notes that caption–image agreement was not human-reviewed, which bounds how precisely prompts are followed. Counting, text and complex scenes are out of reach.
  • No ablations yet. Each architectural choice above is a reasoned design decision, not a measured win. The hyperparameters are sensible defaults, not tuned.
  • CLIP score is a weak metric at this resolution. Judge the model mostly with your eyes.
